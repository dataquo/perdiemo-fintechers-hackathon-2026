### 🧩 How it works

Six steps take you from applying to pitching in front of the jury.

1. **Apply.** Register yourself or your team on Luma by 12 October. Every team member, on site or remote, must register. 
1. **Pick your category.** **Enterprise (Pro)** is only for teams that already exist as a team before the event (colleagues from a bank, enterprise or software house). **Community** is for everyone else, including individuals who still need a team. Both compete for the same prizes.
1. **Onboard on Circle.** This is where team formation starts.
1. **Assemble your crew.** 2 to 8 people. Team formation starts on Circle before the event and is finalised on site on Day 1 (11:00-12:00): come with a team or form one there.
1. **Pick a track.** One of 7, applied for on site right after team formation. Inside it, take a partner challenge if the track has one, or propose your own within the track's theme. No switching tracks afterwards. Tip: backed challenges (marked 🔥 in Tracks and challenges) come with data, tools and partner mentors, so you start building faster.
1. **Build and pitch.** Hack from 12:00 on Day 1 to the code freeze at 13:30 on Day 2, then give a 4-minute live demo plus 3-minute jury Q&A. Your team also attends the mandatory AWS Toolbox on Day 1 (12:00-13:30), which runs alongside hacking.

### 📜 Rules

> [!NOTE]
> 

**The core rule:** bring what you have, but be transparent. Pre-prepared apps and code are allowed; you must clearly state what you built during the hackathon, and only that is evaluated. Work on sandboxed or synthetic data, with respect for everyone in the room.


#### 👥 Team rules

- Register as an individual or as a team. Without a team, team formation starts on Circle once you are onboarded: say what you can bring and which tracks you like, then message potential teammates directly. Teams are finalised on site on Day 1, so hacking can start on time.
- Team size is **2 to 8 people**.
- Hybrid rule: at least **1 team member must be on site** in Prague. The rest may join remotely. All members must register.
- One category (Enterprise or Community) and one track per team. No switching tracks after team formation.
- The live demo is on-site presenters only. No remote presenting.

#### 💻 Code and tools

- **Pre-prepared apps and code are allowed.** You can come with an existing app, prototype or code, your own or open source. You must clearly and transparently state what you built during the hackathon, and **only that is evaluated**. This applies in every track.
- You can build on existing libraries, tools and open-source projects, whether they are yours or not.
- Any LLM or model is allowed. AWS Bedrock credits are provided. Open source is encouraged.
- **How to be transparent:** (1) disclose any pre-prepared app or code in advance by replying to our instruction email, a short description is enough; (2) in your repo, mark the starting point at kickoff with a `hackathon-start` tag or commit, and add a **"Built during the hackathon"** section to your README listing what is new; (3) open your demo with one line on what existed before, then show what you built. Judges score only the work after the `hackathon-start` marker, and before the winners are announced a technical judge reviews the commit history of the top teams. Undisclosed or unclear pre-existing work can lead to disqualification.
- **Your existing code stays yours.** Disclosing it means telling the organisers in advance, by replying to our instruction email, that it exists and what it covers. You do not have to publish it or share it with other participants, and only what you build during the event is judged.
- Using AI assistance is expected and encouraged. Disclose it honestly; it is not penalised.

#### 🗄️ Data

- All data is sandboxed or synthetic: realistic payments, banking and back-office datasets, including a TSYS sandbox available across all 7 tracks.
- No real PII, no production systems, no scraping of production systems.
- No cloning of real people's voice, face or identity, even in the deepfake-detection challenges. Synthetic samples only.

#### 🤝 Code of conduct

- Treat everyone (hackers, mentors, jury, staff, partners) with respect. No harassment, discrimination or disruptive behaviour, on site or on Circle.
- Play fair: credit any open-source or pre-existing tools you build on, and be clear about what you built during the hackathon.
- Report concerns to a FinTechers staff member on site or in the Circle community space. They are handled promptly and privately.
- Breaking these rules can mean disqualification, at the organisers' discretion.

### 🧩 Challenges


#### 🧾 The self-reconciling merchant

**❓ Question:** Can an AI agent reconcile a merchant's daily transactions (payouts, fees, refunds, chargebacks) without human intervention, and explain every match it makes?

**🛠️ Build:** An agent that ingests the TSYS sandbox dataset, reconciles it against simulated bank statements, flags exceptions and produces an auditable explanation log.

**✅ A strong solution shows:** every match explained, exceptions routed to a human with a reason, and an audit log a finance team could hand to an auditor.


#### 💬 Conversational payment operations

**❓ Question:** Can a merchant run payment operations ("why did this transaction fail?", "refund all orders from customer X", "what were my fees last month?") through natural language, safely?

**🛠️ Build:** A copilot over the TSYS data with guardrails: read vs. write actions, confirmation flows and an audit trail for every agent-initiated operation.

**✅ A strong solution shows:** the agent never executes a write action without confirmation, and every answer cites the transactions it is based on.


#### 🛒 Fraud-aware agentic checkout

**❓ Question:** When AI agents shop on behalf of humans, how does a merchant tell a legitimate buyer-agent from a fraud bot?

**🛠️ Build:** A prototype agent-to-merchant checkout protocol with intent verification, spending mandates and real-time risk scoring from sandbox transaction patterns.

**✅ A strong solution shows:** a clear mandate model (who authorised what, up to which limit) and a risk score you can explain.

### ⭐ Partner challenge


#### ⭐ Crypto-asset reserve monitoring · ČNB

> [!NOTE]
> 

Partner challenge by the **Czech National Bank (ČNB)**: "Crypto-asset reserve monitoring: a system for monitoring crypto-asset backing". Full brief attached below, in English and the Czech original. Fits Track 2 tooling: Tatum for on-chain data, Solana for a fast reference chain and price feeds.

**❓ Question:** Stablecoin liabilities move on-chain every second, but reserves are reported periodically. Can you build a SupTech tool that gives the issuer, its partners, its customers or its supervisor a continuously updated view of crypto-asset backing under MiCA, and flags a drop below the threshold in time?

**🛠️ Build:** A working prototype that connects simulated on-chain and off-chain data, calculates the current backing ratio, records the time and origin of every input, and shows the result on a clear dashboard. Document the architecture, data model, assumptions, interface security and how you verify the calculations and alerts.

**Phase 1 · EMT (e-money tokens), 1:1 liquidity check:**

- **On-chain liability aggregation:** query blockchain explorer or node APIs at configurable intervals for tokens in circulation; log source, time, network ID and query success for every fetch.
- **Identifier unification:** identify tokens across DLT networks with the DTI (Digital Token Identifier), not ISIN.
- **Off-chain assets:** a REST endpoint that simulates secure receipt of balances from protected accounts at depositary banks.
- **Reconciliation engine:** a data model and computation layer (e.g. PostgreSQL and Python) linking real-time on-chain data with periodic bank data; show each source's last update, the backing ratio and whether the data is current enough.
- **Crisis alerting:** an executive dashboard that raises an alarm below 1:1, showing the value, calculation time, input recency and source of the deviation.
**Phase 2 · ART (asset-referenced tokens), dynamic portfolio valuation:**

- **Reserve assets:** short-term government bonds (MMF standards), physically allocated precious metals, fiat deposits in several currencies across several banks, and highly liquid on-chain crypto collateral.
- **Granular data model:** asset sub-classes with valuation method, interest rate and maturity.
- **Oracle integration:** market price feeds (mark-to-market) to revalue commodities and bonds in real time.
- **Optional:** own-funds check (at least EUR 350,000, or a percentage of reserve assets, per MiCA, with the rule documented) and asset quality labels traceable to inputs and rules.
**✅ A strong solution shows:** a correct, auditable backing calculation; sensible handling of inputs arriving at different frequencies; good risk signalling; secure integration interfaces; a dashboard a supervisor understands at a glance; and a clear path from EMT to ART.

**🧰 Suggested tools:** Tatum (one API across chains, so on-chain aggregation is a single integration; free credits), Solana devnet and Pyth price feeds for the oracle part, Etherscan or other explorers, Amazon Bedrock. Testnets and simulated balances only.

**📦 Data:** no dataset is handed out. Teams simulate the on-chain supply and the bank balances themselves, as the brief asks.

### 🤖 Solana MCP for your coding agents (guide by Solana CZE/SK)

Connect your coding agent (Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, VS Code, Windsurf) to the official Solana MCP. It gives the agent **current Solana docs** and a **checker for Anchor and Pinocchio program code**. No API key. It does not do wallets, signing or deploys. Mentor: Milan Jandik (Solana CZE/SK). The full PDF guide (10 pages, incl. the `check_solana.sh` script) is shared on Circle.

| Setting | Value |
| URL | `https://mcp.solana.com/mcp` |
| Transport | Streamable HTTP |

#### 1. Connect

- **Claude Code:** `claude mcp add --transport http solana-mcp https://mcp.solana.com/mcp` (add `--scope project` to share it with the team via `.mcp.json`)
- **Codex:** `codex mcp add solana-mcp --url https://mcp.solana.com/mcp`, then `codex mcp list`
- **Cursor:** in `.cursor/mcp.json` add `{"mcpServers": {"solana-mcp": {"url": "https://mcp.solana.com/mcp"}}}`
- **VS Code (Copilot agent mode):** in `.vscode/mcp.json` add `{"servers": {"solana-mcp": {"type": "http", "url": "https://mcp.solana.com/mcp"}}}`, or run *MCP: Add Server* and choose HTTP
- **Windsurf:** in Cascade MCP settings add the server under `mcpServers` with the URL in `serverUrl`
**Check it works:** start a new agent session and ask it to list the Solana MCP tools. You should see `program_autofixer`, `Solana_Documentation_Search`, `Solana_Expert__Ask_For_Help`, `list_sections` and `get_documentation` (some clients add a prefix).


#### 2. Set up your agents

Put project rules in your agent's instruction file (`CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md` for Codex, `.cursor/rules/solana.mdc` for Cursor). Key rules from the guide:

- For anything Solana, use the Solana MCP tools before answering from memory (`list_sections` first, then `get_documentation`).
- After writing or changing program Rust, run `program_autofixer`, fix critical and high issues, and repeat until `require_another_tool_call_after_fixing` is false.
- Never ask for or use private keys, seed phrases or production credentials. Don't deploy or sign anything: a human does that.
- Running several agents? One agent per folder ("your lane"), every task self-contained, and "done" only with proof (command + output, test, commit).

#### 3. Check your program yourself too

The checker finds mechanical problems (e.g. unchecked math) but **not missing access control**. On every instruction that moves value or changes state, check: signers (or `has_one = authority`), account ownership (`Account<'info, T>`, every `UncheckedAccount` has a real `/// CHECK:`), PDA seeds and bump match the client, a wrong-signer test that fails, and token transfers with checked mint, token program, owner and checked math.


#### 4. If the tools don't show up

Use `check_solana.sh` from the PDF (attached inside it) to call the server directly: `./check_solana.sh --list` or `./check_solana.sh $(find programs -name '*.rs')`. Needs bash, curl and node. Exit code 0 = no critical/high issues, 1 = issues found, 2 = check didn't complete (treat as failed).

#### ⭐ AML transaction monitoring: triage and red-flag escalation · FINREG PARTNERS

**❓ Question:** AML transaction monitoring is only part of the puzzle. Every red flag has to be processed, analysed and escalated where necessary. Red flags heavily outnumber the capacity of AML staff, many are false positives, and the real ones have to reach the right person in time while keeping conflicts of interest at bay. Can an automated system save AML staff time, relay important flags to the right people and make every decision explainable, justified and auditable?

**🛠️ Build:** an automated system that (i) generates red flags using the ruleset below and (ii) sorts, rates and escalates each red flag to the right person. Convert all amounts to CZK. Client volume is the sum of all of a client's transactions regardless of direction (in/out), unless the briefing says otherwise.


#### 🚩 Red-flag ruleset

| Type | Rule | Alert unit |
| 🅰️ **A · Client profile discrepancy** | Daily volume in July 2026 is greater than 25% of the projected monthly volume, or greater than 3x the average daily volume in Q2 2026; or a single transaction is greater than 2x the maximum transaction in Q2 2026. Clients inactive in Q2 2026 are compared to projected volumes only | One alert per client |
| 🅱️ **B · High-risk jurisdiction / PEP** | A transaction above CZK 10,000 where the payer, payee or client country is on the EU or FATF high-risk jurisdictions list; or an outgoing transaction of a Politically Exposed Person above CZK 100,000 (where direction is unclear, any transaction) | One alert per transaction |
| ©️ **C · Flow-through account** | Within 24 hours the client receives incoming payments above CZK 100,000 and within 6 hours sends out at least 80% of that amount to another account (not the payer's account). Include any further red flags you identify | One alert per client |

#### ⚙️ For every red flag, the system must

1. **Triage:** false positive or needs human review, with the specific basis (client profile, history, AML risk score, client type, relationship specifics). No red flag is decided without a decision log.
1. **Priority 0-100** from risk factors: AML risk score, PEP, country, amount or volume, conduct discrepancies, length of the client relationship, how the contract was concluded, multiple flags on the same client.
1. **Assign the person responsible** for the final decision (escalation matrix and conflict-of-interest rules are given at the briefing).
1. **Log the final decision.**

### ⭐ Partner challenge


#### ⭐ Automating back-office document controls · Komerční banka

> [!NOTE]
> 

Challenge by **Komerční banka**: from incoming documentation to an auditable result and exception management. Also fits Track 4. Full brief attached below.

**❓ Question:** Can an AI agent take over a diverse set of back-office documents, recognise their type, extract key data, verify completeness and consistency, and pass only the exceptions to a human, without losing the audit trail?

**🎯 Context:** back-office teams check documents by hand, compare data across supporting materials and chase missing or ambiguous information. Build a generally applicable solution that speeds up the check, makes it more consistent and produces a traceable control protocol. The AI output supports the decision; it is not an automatic final approval.

**🛠️ Expected prototype:**

- Accept a package of documents for one business case.
- Check the set is complete: about 5-7 different documents, always defined in the "Contract" document. Classify each document, then extract the pre-defined attributes for its type, with a confidence level.
- If something is missing or specific inconsistencies appear, automatically trigger a request to the client to supplement or correct.
- Sort results into passed checks, discrepancies, warnings and manual review, and generate a cited, auditable protocol with a recommended next step for the back-office worker.
**⚙️ Technical tasks:**

- **Ingestion and preprocessing:** load PDFs and images, OCR, keep the link to the original page.
- **Classification:** determine the document type. Bonus: split multi-document PDFs and handle shuffled pages.
- **Extraction:** structured attributes with source citation and confidence score.
- **Rules engine:** configurable checks for completeness, consistency and logical links.
- **Human in the loop:** confirm, edit, reject or send back for supplementation.
- **Audit trail:** input versions, applied rule or model, run timestamp, history of human interventions.
**🎬 Demo scenario:** a happy path (a complete set passes and a protocol is generated), an error path (a document is missing, or a key data item is missing or doesn't match), and the automatic resolution or human next steps.

**📊 Evaluated on:** accuracy of classification, extraction and checks; traceability of evidence and explainability; robustness to document quality and variants; real-world usability for the back office.

**🚧 Guardrails:** rules must be separate from extraction and easy to change; the rules should improve continuously from real decisions.

### 🔑 Signing in the first time

1. **Open the portal and enter your email.** Use the address you registered with. There is no password to find; you have never been given one.
1. **Collect the one-time code from your inbox.** Your first sign-in attempt triggers the email, so nothing is waiting before that. The code is short-lived; if you leave it an hour, start again.
1. **Register an authenticator app.** Scan the QR code with Google Authenticator, 1Password, Authy or similar. You need it every time you sign in, so use a device you have at the venue.
1. **Choose your account and role.** You see your team's account with `ai-hackathon-TeamAdmin`, plus read-only access to a shared account with datasets. Pick TeamAdmin.
> [!NOTE]
> 

**Set your region immediately.** The console remembers the last region you used, and the region selector is the most common cause of mysterious permission errors. Set **Europe (Frankfurt) eu-central-1** before you create anything.

### 🤖 Calling models on Bedrock

Model access is already enabled. Nothing to request.


#### Claude

In Europe, Claude runs through EU cross-region inference profiles, so the model ID needs the `**eu.**`** prefix**. A bare `anthropic.claude-…` ID will not work. Prices per million tokens (input / output). Rate limits are shared across your team.

| Model ID | Requests/min | In / out | Tokens/min |
| `eu.anthropic.claude-haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0` | 50 | USD 1.10 / 5.50 | 5M |
| `eu.anthropic.claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929-v1:0` | 50 | USD 3.30 / 16.50 | 5M |
| `eu.anthropic.claude-opus-4-5-20251101-v1:0` | 25 | USD 5.50 / 27.50 | 2M |
> [!NOTE]
> 

**Claude 5 is not available, don't lose time on it.** Sonnet 5, Opus 5 and Sonnet 5.5 have no capacity in this region and return "AccessDeniedException … not available for this account". A mentor cannot switch this on. Use Sonnet 4.5 or Opus 4.5.


#### Everything else

No prefix needed, effectively unlimited throughput (100M tokens/min), good for high-volume loops and evaluation runs: `qwen.qwen3-235b-a22b-2507-v1:0`, `qwen.qwen3-32b-v1:0`, `qwen.qwen3-coder-30b-a3b-v1:0`, `zai.glm-4.7-flash`, `nvidia.nemotron-super-3-120b`, `mistral.devstral-2-123b`, `minimax.minimax-m2.5`, `openai.gpt-oss-120b-1:0`, `openai.gpt-oss-20b-1:0`.

Amazon Nova and Pixtral use EU profiles with lower limits: `eu.amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0` (8M), `eu.amazon.nova-pro-v1:0` (2M), `eu.amazon.nova-micro-v1:0` and `eu.amazon.nova-lite-v1:0` (400K), `eu.mistral.pixtral-large-2502-v1:0` (80K).


#### Voice: Nova Sonic, the one exception to Frankfurt

Speech-to-speech models are only in Stockholm: `amazon.nova-2-sonic-v1:0` (speech in, speech + text out) and `amazon.nova-2-5-sonic` (speech + text in, speech + text out), both with `--region eu-north-1`. They use the bidirectional streaming API (`InvokeModelWithBidirectionalStream`), not Converse.

> [!NOTE]
> 

**Keep your data in Frankfurt.** Only the model call goes to Stockholm. S3 buckets, Lambdas and everything else stay in eu-central-1. Transcribe and Polly are blocked in eu-north-1; if you want Transcribe, run it in eu-central-1.


#### A first call

```bash
aws bedrock-runtime converse \
  --region eu-central-1 \
  --model-id eu.anthropic.claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929-v1:0 \
  --messages '[{"role":"user","content":[{"text":"Say hello"}]}]' \
  --inference-config '{"maxTokens":100}'
```

In Python: `boto3.client("bedrock-runtime", region_name="eu-central-1")` and the same `converse` call. To see everything available: `aws bedrock list-inference-profiles --region eu-central-1`.

> [!NOTE]
> 

**Prompt caching is the cheapest optimisation you will make.** Cached input tokens cost ten times less (USD 0.33 vs 3.30 per million on Sonnet 4.5). If your agent resends the same system prompt or document every turn, cache it.

### 💰 Watching your spend

Your team has **USD 800**, shared across everyone in your account. Nothing cuts you off: the budget alerts the organisers, it does not stop you.

> [!NOTE]
> 

**The billing console will not open, use the CLI.** Cost Explorer in the browser is switched off at account level, so don't ask for permissions. The data is available over the API (in `us-east-1`, the one exception to Frankfurt):

```bash
# What have we spent?
aws ce get-cost-and-usage --region us-east-1 \
  --time-period Start=2026-10-01,End=2026-10-21 \
  --granularity MONTHLY --metrics UnblendedCost \
  --query 'ResultsByTime[0].Total.UnblendedCost.Amount' --output text

# Which model is eating the budget?
aws ce get-cost-and-usage --region us-east-1 \
  --time-period Start=2026-10-01,End=2026-10-21 \
  --granularity MONTHLY --metrics UnblendedCost \
  --group-by Type=DIMENSION,Key=SERVICE \
  --query 'ResultsByTime[0].Groups[?Metrics.UnblendedCost.Amount!=`0`].[Keys[0],Metrics.UnblendedCost.Amount]' \
  --output table
```

- You only see your own account. No team sees another's spend.
- Figures lag by up to 24 hours. It is a review tool, not a live meter.
- Amounts under a cent show as 0. On day one it looks like nothing is recorded; it is.
- Output tokens dominate. Draft with Haiku and escalate to Opus only where it matters.

### 🚧 What's blocked, and why

Guardrails are enforced at account level, so an error is final. There is no local workaround.

1. **Regions other than Frankfurt.** Everything you create lives in eu-central-1. A tutorial that says us-east-1 needs changing.
1. **IAM users and access keys.** Long-lived keys leaked in public repos are the classic hackathon incident. Use IAM roles instead (allowed).
1. **Large and GPU instances.** Available: t3.*, t4g.*, m6i.large, m6i.xlarge, m7g.large, m7g.xlarge, c6i.large, c7g.large. Inference runs on Bedrock, so no GPU is needed; talk to a mentor first if you think you need one.
1. **Services that bill while idle.** Reserved instances, capacity reservations, savings plans, dedicated hosts, Redshift, MSK/Kafka, EMR, Outposts, Snowball, Braket, domain registration and Shield Advanced are unavailable.
> [!NOTE]
> 

**Vector stores are open, with one cost trap.** OpenSearch, RDS with pgvector and Bedrock Knowledge Bases are available. A provisioned `t3.small.search` OpenSearch domain costs about USD 1 a day; **OpenSearch Serverless costs about USD 23 a day** whether you query it or not. Pick provisioned, and delete it before you leave.


### 🛠️ When something fails

| What you see | What it means | Fix |
| "explicit deny in a service control policy", ARN shows a region other than eu-central-1 | Console or CLI pointed at the wrong region | You: switch to Frankfurt and retry |
| "explicit deny in a service control policy", ARN says eu-central-1 | A gap in the guardrails, not your mistake | Organiser: report it with the full message |
| "no instances found" during `aws configure sso` | SSO region entered as eu-central-1 | You: it is eu-north-1 |
| "not available for this account" on a Claude 5 model | No capacity in Europe | You: use Sonnet 4.5 or Opus 4.5 |
| ValidationException on `anthropic.claude-…` | Missing the EU profile prefix | You: prepend `eu.` |
| ThrottlingException | Team hit the shared limit (50 requests/min, 25 on Opus) | You: back off, stagger jobs, move bulk work to Qwen or Nova |
| Cannot create an access key | Blocked by design | You: `aws sso login`, or a role for running code |
| Credentials stopped working | The 12-hour session ended | You: `aws sso login` again |
| Cost Explorer shows 0 | Sub-cent rounding or the 24-hour lag | You: normal, check later |
When you ask for help, paste the complete error message: the region in the ARN and the exact action name make it diagnosable in seconds.
